## Q2. What wins AI/startup hackathons (Korea + global), and why teams lose

### Takeaway
Judges reward **a real, specific user problem, plus one stable, working "wow" flow, plus a story a non-engineer can follow, plus (in startup-framed or government-backed contests) a believable revenue path**. Teams lose by over-indexing on one criterion (all backend, no UI), by showing buggy breadth instead of stable depth, by explaining architecture instead of value, and by repeating templates or rehashed ideas.

### Cited Findings
**Korean winner retrospectives and contest outcomes:**
- The 2025 서울 AI 해커톤 대상 winner (박지성, 한신대) says to fit the pitch to the contest type. For development contests, show the engineering and problem-solving process. For contests that look at business viability and profitability, **explain the revenue structure well**. **Government-affiliated hackathons "사업성을 꼭 본다."** — [velog 후기](https://velog.io/@jisung719/%EC%84%9C%EC%9A%B8-AI-%ED%95%B4%EC%BB%A4%ED%86%A4-%EC%88%98%EC%83%81-%ED%9B%84%EA%B8%B0-1%EC%9C%84-%ED%8C%81)
- Same author: **know who the judges are**, review past winners, and make the presentation understandable "개발자가 아닌 사람도, 초등학생도" (even to non-developers or children). Don't over-emphasize architecture. Spend the most time on **choosing the idea**, align it with the stated criteria, and make data collection organic rather than forced. Format: 3-person team, initial pitch → 20 teams → AWS training → 10 finalists. The team won 대상 (서울시장상) and ₩20M for "서울 메아리", a public platform that detects potholes and trash from citizen photos — [velog 후기](https://velog.io/@jisung719/%EC%84%9C%EC%9A%B8-AI-%ED%95%B4%EC%BB%A4%ED%86%A4-%EC%88%98%EC%83%81-%ED%9B%84%EA%B8%B0-1%EC%9C%84-%ED%8C%81); [한신대 보도](https://www.hs.ac.kr/bbs/kor/277/151725/artclView)
- The 2025 SW 마이스터 연합 해커톤 대상 (과기정통부 장관상) went to a **prompt-injection detection tool for AI browsers**, a timely AI-native problem rather than a generic chatbot — [velog 후기](https://velog.io/@dino0204/2025-SW-%EB%A7%88%EC%9D%B4%EC%8A%A4%ED%84%B0-%EC%97%B0%ED%95%A9-%ED%95%B4%EC%BB%A4%ED%86%A4-%EB%8C%80%EC%83%81-%ED%9B%84%EA%B8%B0) (search snippet)
- At the 2026 대학(원)생 서비스업 창업 아이디어 공모전 (한국서비스경영학회, 2026-05-08), the 대상 went to **"FAN;FARE", an AI platform that runs birthday-cafe operations** (venue booking, goods production, scheduling) for a niche fan subculture. Other winners: an accessible travel-companion matcher, a real-time vacant-seat alert ("SMART SEAT FLOW"), an AI 전세사기 prevention housing platform, and AI demand-forecast ingredient auto-ordering. Judges valued "addressing actual user problems considering market viability, feasibility, and scalability" — [머니투데이 2026-05-12](https://www.mt.co.kr/future/2026/05/12/2026051214124521640)
- The 2026 AI·Blockchain Business Model Competition (2026-05-11) drew 64 teams from 58 universities, from which 15 finalists were chosen. It was judged by 14 experts (investment directors, fintech specialists, AI researchers) on **creativity, technical capability, logical soundness, and practical applicability**. An organizer stressed that in regulated sectors "the implementation pathway for business ideas is crucial." One 대상 was a B2B SaaS that helps VC/AC reviewers with due diligence — [헤럴드경제](https://biz.heraldcorp.com/article/10736228)
- **PSST is the standard Korean government startup-evaluation frame** (예비/초기창업패키지): Problem (문제인식), Solution (실현가능성), Scale-up (성장전략), Team (팀구성). In 서류심사, **Problem and Solution carry the most weight**, and Team differentiates less. Practitioners advise describing the problem as a phenomenon from the target customer's view and showing why existing products fail. Solution is "the most important part", centered on the core value delivered and what has been built so far — [genDOC PSST guide](https://gendoc.ai/blog/psst-business-plan-guide); [brunch PSST 작성법](https://brunch.co.kr/@asdkorea1811/23)
- Scale: 멋쟁이사자처럼 14기 중앙해커톤 (2026) had about 2,500 students from 80 universities building AI services. The campus AI-service space is crowded, so differentiation matters — [Nate/news 2026-08-27](https://m.news.nate.com/view/20260827n26369)

**Global judge advice (Devpost, five judges):**
- Richard Moot (Square): rewards projects that **balance all criteria**. The common failure is "over-indexed on one particular criteria", e.g. backend-heavy work with minimal UI. Understand the audience before building — [Devpost](https://info.devpost.com/blog/hackathon-judging-tips)
- Karen Bajza-Terlouw (Databricks): "Storytelling component is huge." She penalizes ambiguity and **polished videos masking weak code** — [Devpost](https://info.devpost.com/blog/hackathon-judging-tips)
- Kelvin Boateng (Google): favors playable demos and departures from templates. He penalizes submissions that return the template with cosmetic changes — [Devpost](https://info.devpost.com/blog/hackathon-judging-tips)
- Maria Yarotska (NEAR): looks for an **entrepreneurial mindset**, fresh approaches and "the fire in their eyes". She is wary of recycled projects and oversized teams — [Devpost](https://info.devpost.com/blog/hackathon-judging-tips)
- Warren Marusiak (Atlassian): wants polished, working products that **directly address the prompt**. Rehashed or oversimplified ideas and unmet requirements lose — [Devpost](https://info.devpost.com/blog/hackathon-judging-tips)
- Generic guide: "A simple, stable demo of one amazing feature is infinitely better than a complex, buggy demo of ten half-finished ones." A project for a specific community beats a clever demo without a purpose — [placementpreparation.io](https://www.placementpreparation.io/blog/how-to-win-a-hackathon/)

### Inferences
- **Losing patterns, synthesized:** (1) a generic "ChatGPT for X" with no proprietary workflow, data or distribution; (2) demoing 5 shaky features instead of 1 bulletproof flow; (3) a talk dominated by architecture diagrams; (4) a pain point the team assumed rather than evidenced; (5) a vague "광고 수익 / 구독" money slide with no price point or payer; (6) ignoring the stated theme. Here, that means not tying back to WHO/AI/MONEY.
- For "귀찮음 주식회사", niche and concrete beats broad. FAN;FARE shows a subculture-specific workflow (birthday cafés) can beat broad platforms. Pick a narrow, recurring, slightly embarrassing chore with a clear payer.
- The "wrapper" critique is best answered by showing what the LLM *can't* do alone: integration into the student's actual channel (KakaoTalk, calendar), proprietary or structured campus data, an action taken on the user's behalf, or a human-in-the-loop marketplace.

## Q3. Pitch structure for WHO / AI / MONEY: evidencing pain and willingness to pay

### Takeaway
Map the three boxes onto PSST: WHO = Problem, AI = Solution, MONEY = Scale-up. Evidence beats assertion. The strongest moves within a hackathon window are (a) live micro-research with real students, reported with a number, (b) a demand signal people have to *act* on (waitlist signups, a 동아리 LOI, a test-mode payment), and (c) one unit-economics slide with an explicit price, cost per use and margin.

### Cited Findings
- PSST guidance: open Problem with the **target customer's own severe pain** and **why existing solutions fail**. Solution should center on the core value and **what has actually been built** — [genDOC](https://gendoc.ai/blog/psst-business-plan-guide); [brunch](https://brunch.co.kr/@asdkorea1811/23)
- The Seoul AI Hackathon winner stresses aligning with evaluation criteria and making data collection "organic". The startup-type contest explicitly checks the 수익구조 — [velog 후기](https://velog.io/@jisung719/%EC%84%9C%EC%9A%B8-AI-%ED%95%B4%EC%BB%A4%ED%86%A4-%EC%88%98%EC%83%81-%ED%9B%84%EA%B8%B0-1%EC%9C%84-%ED%8C%81)
- **Caution on using 에브리타임 content as evidence:** 에브리타임 rules forbid leaking posts (copying or screenshots), and violations can bring a 5-year ban. Users who try to crawl it report having their accounts blocked — [나무위키 에브리타임 문제점](https://namu.wiki/w/%EC%97%90%EB%B8%8C%EB%A6%AC%ED%83%80%EC%9E%84/%EB%AC%B8%EC%A0%9C%EC%A0%90%20%EB%B0%8F%20%EC%82%AC%EA%B1%B4%20%EC%82%AC%EA%B3%A0) (secondary source)
- Cluely's viral launch shows how demand signals work in pitches: 70,000 signups in the first week, cited alongside funding. Its founder later admitted an inflated ARR figure ($7M claimed vs. $5.2M actual), which shows that **faked or inflated traction backfires** — [Wikipedia: Cluely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluely)
- Toss Payments test mode lets a team run a **real-looking checkout without a 사업자번호 or even sign-up**, using public document test keys (details in Q4). This makes a "pay now" button in the demo feasible — [Toss Payments blog](https://docs.tosspayments.com/blog/how-to-test-toss-payments)

### Inferences
Suggested slide skeleton (5 minutes):
1. **WHO (40s):** one named persona plus a real quote from someone interviewed today, e.g. "국민대 3학년, 팀플 4개, 회의록 정리 매주 2시간". Add one number from the team's own micro-survey (an n=30 Google Form posted to a 과 단톡방 or 동아리 채팅, with the method stated). Post the survey *link* rather than scraping or screenshotting 에타.
2. **AI (90s, mostly live demo):** show "before" (the manual chore, timed) and then "after" (the AI does it end to end). Show one moment the user could not get from plain ChatGPT, such as reading their actual schedule, posting into their KakaoTalk, or filing a form.
3. **MONEY (60s):** who pays (student, 학생회/동아리, 학과 사무실, or a B2B sponsor), the price point (e.g. ₩2,900/month or ₩500 per task), the cost per task (LLM tokens + STT/OCR minutes; see Q4), gross margin, and the first 100 paying users and where they come from.
4. **Proof (30s):** waitlist count collected during the event, a 동아리 회장's signed or photographed LOI ("다음 학기 회비로 결제 의향"), and a test-mode payment completed by a real student.
5. **Why us / next 30 days (20s).**
- **Unit economics on one slide:** price minus (LLM cost per task × tasks per month + STT/OCR + payment fee) = contribution margin, plus CAC through free channels (학과 단톡, 동아리 연합회). The Q4 numbers make the AI cost per task concrete. For example, ~10K input + 1K output tokens on Claude Sonnet 5.5 at $2/$10 per MTok is about $0.03.
- B2B2C payers (학생회, 동아리 연합, 학과 사무실, 학교 창업지원단) often make a more credible MONEY box than individual students, who are price-sensitive.

### Gaps
- No Korean source was found that quantifies how judges weight pre-sales or LOIs at student hackathons. The advice above is inferred from PSST and general startup practice.
- Survey-response norms for 단톡방/에타 posts (typical n within 24h) were not found.

## Q4. Tech feasibility menu for a ~24–48h build (as of late 2026)

### Takeaway
Within 24–48 hours a small team can reliably ship an LLM-centric web app, a Telegram or Discord bot, receipt/document OCR, Korean STT, Google Sheets/Calendar integration for a handful of test users, scraping of public notice pages, and a Toss Payments test checkout. The risky items are anything that needs **business verification or third-party approval**: KakaoTalk 알림톡 (needs a 사업자 business channel plus template review), the Kakao i 오픈빌더 chatbot (OBT approval), and Google OAuth beyond 100 test users. The same goes for anything that **logs into school SSO or 에브리타임 on users' behalf**, because of ToS, legal and personal-data exposure.

### Cited Findings
**LLM APIs:**
- **Claude** (Anthropic API, model table cached 2026-09-25): Claude Opus 5.5 `claude-opus-5-5` at $4/$20 per MTok in/out with 1M context. Claude Sonnet 5.5 `claude-sonnet-5-5` at $2/$10 with 1M context. Claude Haiku 4.5 `claude-haiku-4-5` at $1/$5 with 200K context. PDF input is native (base64 or Files API, up to 32 MB per request and 600 pages). Structured outputs (`output_config.format`), server-side web search/fetch and code execution are built in. **Computer use** is available, but on the Claude API it runs only through the `computer_toolset_20260801` tool for Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5. Forced `tool_choice` is rejected on these models, so teams should use `auto` plus `strict: true` or structured outputs — Source: Claude API skill reference bundled with Claude Code (cached 2026-09-25); canonical page [Anthropic models overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/overview)
- **Gemini** free tier: rate-limited per project in AI Studio. Aggregator figures show 2.5 Flash at about 10 RPM / 500 RPD and Flash-Lite at about 30 RPM / 1,500 RPD. Several 2026 sources say **Pro models left the free tier in April 2026**, though others claim limited Pro access remains. Google does not guarantee the figures — [tinkerllm](https://tinkerllm.com/blog/gemini-api-free-tier-limits-rate-quotas/); [memetik (Sept 2026)](https://www.memetik.ai/guides/gemini-api-free-tier-limits). The sources conflict and are not primary, so treat the free tier as fine for development but too thin for a public demo spike.
- Separately, some Korean universities advertised a **Gemini 1-year free student plan** in 2026 (consumer app, not API) — [동양미래대 공지](https://www.dongyang.ac.kr/bbs/dmu/677/251648/artclView.do)

**Korean speech-to-text:**
- **RTZR (리턴제로) STT**: from ₩1,000/hr. **NAVER CLOVA Speech**: ₩1,200/hr, billed in 15-second units rounded up. Published Korean CER is RTZR 4.66% vs. CLOVA 9.09% — [Soniox Korea comparison](https://soniox.com/korea). This is a *competitor's* marketing page, which claims Soniox is about 7–8× cheaper, so treat it with caution.
- RTZR's own benchmark ranks its engines (including a RTZR Whisper variant) 1st and 2nd at under 8% average error, ahead of CLOVA. That is a **vendor self-benchmark** — [RTZR blog](https://blog.rtzr.ai/korean-speechai-benchmark/); [RTZR developers](https://developers.rtzr.ai/); [rtzr/Awesome-Korean-Speech-Recognition](https://github.com/rtzr/Awesome-Korean-Speech-Recognition)

**OCR and document parsing:**
- **Upstage Document Parse**: Standard $0.01/page, Enhanced $0.03/page, with an Auto mode. Receipt key-field extraction (vendor, amount, date) via Parse + Extract costs about $0.04/page, excluding 10% VAT — [Upstage pricing](https://www.upstage.ai/pricing); [Upstage Document Parse](https://www.upstage.ai/products/document-parse)
- Multimodal LLMs (Claude PDF and image input, above) can also extract receipt fields directly into a JSON schema, which removes a vendor dependency for a demo — [Anthropic models overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/overview) (per skill reference)

**KakaoTalk:**
- **알림톡 requires a 카카오 비즈니스 채널.** Converting to a business channel takes about 2–3 business days according to one source and 3–7 according to another. It needs a **사업자등록증** plus other documents. After approval, **each 알림톡 template must be registered and reviewed separately**, and a 고객센터 number must be registered before the 발신 프로필 키 is issued. One guide titles the whole setup "1주일 만에 세팅" — [BIZGO blog](https://blog.bizgo.io/howto/how-to-send-alimtalk/); [BIZGO 비즈니스 채널 2026](https://blog.bizgo.io/howto/create-kakaotalk-business-channel-guide-2026/); [m8 blog](https://getm8.ai/blog/kakaobusiness-channel-setup); [NHN Cloud 알림톡 가이드](https://docs.nhncloud.com/ko/Notification/Notification%20Hub/ko/service-policy-and-precondition/alimtalk-and-friendtalk/)
- **Kakao i 오픈빌더 (chatbot)** requires an **OBT application approved by Kakao**. The review considers internal criteria such as whether the applicant has run a channel before, and results arrive by email. **Practical rules:**
- Pre-record a backup demo video *and* run a live demo with seeded data. Pin API keys to a server-side proxy, never the client.

## Q5. Recent (2025–2026) student-built AI services that went viral or got funded

### Takeaway
Few hackathon-to-funding stories involving Korean students could be verified. The verifiable examples are a teenage founder's AI startup (셀론) with seed money from Primer and The Ventures, award-winning niche AI service concepts in 2026 university contests, and globally Cluely, a student-built product with a viral, controversial launch that raised $20.3M. What they share is **a sharp, specific pain plus aggressive distribution**, not technical novelty.

### Cited Findings
- **셀론 (Korea, 2026-04):** founder 장준하, **age 18**, raised **seed funding from 프라이머 and 더벤처스** for "메모리", a solution that improves AI answer quality. He had spent 3 years quickly finding, validating and iterating on real customer problems — [Nate/news 2026-04-29](https://m.news.nate.com/view/20260429n28016)
- **FAN;FARE (2026-05):** 대상 at the 대학(원)생 서비스업 창업 아이디어 공모전 for an AI birthday-cafe operations platform, a niche fandom workflow — [머니투데이](https://www.mt.co.kr/future/2026/05/12/2026051214124521640)
- **서울 메아리 (2025):** student team "리틀브라더" won 대상 and ₩20M at 서울 AI 해커톤 with AI detection of potholes and trash from citizen photos — [한신대](https://www.hs.ac.kr/bbs/kor/277/151725/artclView)
- **Kookmin students (2026):** swept the SEA:ME 해커톤 top three using vibe coding, and took 2nd globally in the IBM Bob hackathon — [스마트경제](http://www.dailysmart.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=128021); [Nate/news](https://m.news.nate.com/view/20260629n27785)
- **Cluely (US, 2025):** Columbia students Roy Lee and Neel Shanmugam were suspended over "Interview Coder" and launched Cluely on 2025-04-20 with the tagline "Cheat on Everything". It got **70,000 signups in week one**, a $5.3M seed (2025-04-21, Abstract/Susa) and a $15M Series A led by a16z (2025-06-20). The founder later admitted overstating ARR ($7M claimed vs. $5.2M), and the company dropped the cheating messaging — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluely)
- Context: 뤼튼 (wrtn), a Korean AI service, raised a ~₩100B Series C at a ₩1T+ valuation (2026-08). It is not student-built but shows a large Korean consumer-AI market — [Nate/news 2026-08-29](https://m.news.nate.com/view/20260829n04300)
- A Chinese student built a "future prediction system" in 10 days and raised about ₩6.4B (2026-03) — [Nate/news 2026-03-17](https://m.news.nate.com/view/20260317n32346) (headline only, not verified in depth)

### Inferences
- The viral pattern is a **provocative, instantly understood promise for one painful moment**, distributed where students already gather. For "귀찮음 주식회사", a deliberately cheeky framing ("교수님 공지 대신 읽어주는 AI", "팀플 무임승차 증거 자동 정리") can help memorability. The Cluely case shows the limit: ethics and academic-integrity backlash. Avoid ideas that help students cheat on exams or assignments.
- Judges and VCs reward **evidence of iteration with real users** (셀론's "3 years of validating customer problems"). In a hackathon, the scaled-down version is to talk to 10 real students and show what changed because of them.
